Flying out of Australia means long distances to almost every major overseas hub, so airfares are a big part of any holiday budget. The good news is that timing your trip well can save you hundreds of dollars per ticket.

Whether you're planning a tropical escape or a long-haul trip, knowing the cheapest time to fly from Australia is one of the easiest ways to stretch your budget. This guide focuses on Australia-specific departure timing – when Australians actually pay the least to fly out – based on Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report and practical seasonal patterns. 1. The Seasonal Breakdown: Which Months Offer the Best Flight Deals?

Airfares follow demand. When lots of Australians want to travel at once – summer school holidays, Christmas, Easter and the mid-year break – prices climb. When demand drops, fares usually follow.

  • February to March: Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report named February the cheapest month of the year for Australians to fly internationally, with average fares up to 74% (around $520) lower than December. Once school goes back, leisure demand cools, which makes late summer and early autumn a good window to look for deals.
  • May to June: The late-autumn and early-winter stretch before the mid-year school holidays is usually quieter, which can make it a good time for short international trips such as New Zealand or Southeast Asia.
  • After the Mid-Year School Holidays: Once the July school holidays finish, demand typically eases again – worth checking if you're after winter sun somewhere warm.
  • October to Early December: The late-spring window before the Christmas rush is another shoulder period worth watching.

School holiday dates differ between states, so check your state's calendar when you plan. For more ways to save, see our guide on how to find cheap flights: smart strategies for Australian travellers.

2. Days of the Week and Time of Day: When Airfares Dip

The month you fly sets the baseline. The day and time you fly can shave off a bit more.

Best Days of the Week to Depart

  • Friday: Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report found Friday was the cheapest day for Australians to depart, with fares up to 12% lower than Sunday.
  • Tuesday: The same report found Tuesday was the quietest day at airports – handy if you'd rather avoid queues.
  • Be Flexible: Day-of-week patterns vary by route and season, so use the flexible-dates or calendar view on Google Flights or Skyscanner to compare fares across a whole week or month.

Best Flight Times

Early-morning and overnight "red-eye" flights are less popular with many travellers, so they're often worth comparing against daytime departures. On busy, competitive routes you'll also usually see more fare options, which can help you find a cheaper time slot.

4. Booking Windows vs Flying Windows: The Sale Factor

Picking the right month is only half the job. When you book matters too.

  • The Booking Sweet Spot: Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report found that booking four to six weeks before departure saved international travellers an average of $140, and domestic travellers around $40, compared with booking six months ahead.
  • When to Book: The same report found July was the cheapest month to make a booking (up to 20% lower than November), and Thursday was the cheapest day of the week to book (up to 7% lower than Saturday).
  • Peak Periods Are Different: For Christmas, Easter and school holiday travel, cheap seats can sell out early. Start watching fares well ahead and set price alerts rather than waiting.
  • Watch Airline Sales: Jetstar runs its Friday Fare Frenzy sales, and other airlines run regular promotions too. For tips on catching low-cost carrier deals, see our essential Jetstar sale tips.

5. Tailored Advice: Flight Strategies by Situation

Scenario A: The Flexible Remote Worker or Solo Traveller

  • Goal: Save as much as possible with no fixed dates.
  • Strategy: Look at February and other shoulder months, compare Friday and midweek departures, and set Google Flights or Skyscanner price alerts so you catch fare drops.

Scenario B: The Family Bound by School Holidays

  • Goal: Keep costs down when you can only travel in the school holidays.
  • Strategy: Start watching fares early, compare different departure days within the holidays, and consider destinations that aren't in their own peak season at the same time.

Scenario C: The Short-Break Escaper

  • Goal: A cheap quick getaway to New Zealand or the Pacific.
  • Strategy: Compare a few different departure and return days. Popular weekend times are often dearer, so shifting your trip by a day or two can make a difference.
