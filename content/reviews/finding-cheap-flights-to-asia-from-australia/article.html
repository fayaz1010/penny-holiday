Picture this: you're at your desk on an overcast afternoon, dreaming of an iced coffee in Ho Chi Minh City or a beachfront sunset in Bali. You open a flight search engine, type in your destination, see a high return fare and close the tab, assuming an affordable trip to Asia is out of reach.

Low-cost airfares from Australia to Asia are very achievable once you understand how airlines structure their routes, schedule their sales and price their seats. This guide covers practical strategies for finding budget flights, the low-cost carrier landscape and the most common booking mistakes to avoid. It builds on our main guide to cheap flights from Australia.

Quick answers

  • Which budget airlines fly from Australia to Asia? Jetstar, Scoot, AirAsia and VietJet all fly low-cost services between Australian cities and Asian hubs such as Denpasar (Bali), Singapore, Kuala Lumpur and Ho Chi Minh City. According to BITRE, low-cost carriers carried 20.9% of all international passengers to and from Australia in July 2026.
  • When is the best time to book flights to Southeast Asia? There's no single magic date, but booking a month or more ahead and avoiding school holidays and other peak periods generally gives you the best shot at lower fares.
  • Can a different departure airport save money? Often, yes. Fares to the same Asian city can vary a lot between Australian airports, so it's worth comparing several departure cities – Perth in particular is much closer to Southeast Asia than the east coast.

The most costly booking mistakes to avoid when flying to Asia

High fares often come down to booking habits rather than a lack of deals. Here are four common mistakes Australian travellers make. The dollar impact of each depends on your route, dates and how flexible you are.

1. Only searching from your home airport

  • Why it happens: Most travellers only search from their nearest capital city airport and assume that's the cheapest option.
  • What to do instead: Compare prices across several Australian airports before you book. Prices for the same Asian destination can differ significantly depending on where you leave from. Perth is the closest major gateway to Southeast Asia, with relatively short flights to Bali and Singapore and plenty of low-cost competition. Victorians can also check Avalon: Jetstar launched direct Avalon–Denpasar flights in March 2026, which may sometimes undercut Tullamarine. If you live elsewhere, an inexpensive domestic flight to a better-connected airport can sometimes unlock international savings – just allow plenty of time between flights. More on this in our main guide to cheap flights from Australia.

2. Getting caught out by baggage and add-on fees

  • Why it happens: Budget carriers advertise eye-catching one-way fares that usually include only a small carry-on allowance (often around 7kg).
  • What to do instead: Work out the total price, including checked bags and seat selection, before you pay. Adding bags or extra carry-on weight at the airport is usually far more expensive than adding them when you book. If you're flying Jetstar, read our Jetstar sale tips to learn how to bundle extras without paying full price.

3. Booking far too early or waiting for a last-minute drop

  • Why it happens: Travellers often fall into one of two traps: booking almost a year ahead out of fear of missing out, or waiting until the final fortnight hoping for a clearance sale.
  • What to do instead: Last-minute fares on popular Asian routes are often higher, not lower, so don't count on a late drop. Start watching fares a few months out, set price alerts, and book when you see a fare that fits your budget – especially for peak periods. For more on timing, read our guide on when the cheapest time to fly from Australia is.

4. Skipping transit hubs and connecting itineraries

  • Why it happens: If you're heading to a smaller destination such as Chiang Mai, Da Nang, Langkawi or Cebu, low-cost search results can look messy, so many people default to a full-service ticket.
  • What to do instead: Look at routing through major Asian low-cost hubs. Scoot (via Singapore), AirAsia (via Kuala Lumpur) and VietJet (via Ho Chi Minh City) all connect to wider networks, and some offer connecting itineraries on a single booking. You can also book two separate low-cost tickets with a generous layover – but if the first flight is delayed, the second airline usually isn't obliged to help, so leave a big buffer. This technique is covered in our guide on how to find cheap flights.

Which low-cost airlines fly from Australia to Asia?

The Australia–Asia corridor has strong low-cost competition. Knowing each airline's network helps you target sales. Networks change often, so always check the airline's current route map before planning around a specific route.

  • VietJet Air: Vietnam's low-cost carrier flies between Vietnam and several Australian cities, including Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane and Perth. The Australian Government has announced that VietJet will fly between Ho Chi Minh City and Western Sydney International Airport, giving travellers in Sydney's west another option.
  • Scoot: Singapore Airlines' low-cost subsidiary flies between Singapore and Australian cities including Sydney, Melbourne and Perth, with onward connections across Southeast Asia, North Asia and beyond.
  • AirAsia & AirAsia X: AirAsia is a major budget player out of Perth and Melbourne. In August 2026 AirAsia announced a network recalibration that ends Sydney–Kuala Lumpur flights from 25 October 2026 and shifts capacity to Perth and Melbourne.
  • Jetstar: The Qantas Group's low-cost brand flies from a number of Australian cities to destinations including Bali, Singapore, Bangkok, Phuket, Ho Chi Minh City and Japan.

How does seasonality affect flight prices to Asia?

Fares from Australia to Asia rise and fall with demand. Peak periods for Australian travellers typically include:

  • School holidays: Including late September–early October, around Easter and April, and mid-December through January.
  • Christmas and New Year.
  • Major Asian holidays: Such as Lunar New Year (late January or February, depending on the year), which can push up fares and accommodation prices in parts of Asia.

To give yourself the best shot at lower fares:

  1. Avoid peak windows where you can: Fares on popular routes usually climb sharply during Australian school holidays and the Christmas–New Year period. If you can travel outside these windows, you'll typically pay less.
  2. Consider shoulder periods: Times between the major holiday peaks – for example, parts of February, March, May and November – can offer better value. Check the weather at your destination too, as monsoon and typhoon seasons vary across Asia.
  3. Set up fare alerts: Prices change daily as seats sell. Setting price alerts a couple of months before your trip means you'll hear quickly when airlines launch flash sales.
