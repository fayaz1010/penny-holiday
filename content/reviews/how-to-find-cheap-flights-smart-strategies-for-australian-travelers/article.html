Key takeaways

  • Understand market concentration: Australia's domestic airline market is dominated by two airline groups, so actively tracking deals is essential for finding cheap airfares.
  • Use comparison sites to discover, then book direct: Use tools like Google Flights and Skyscanner to watch prices, then consider booking directly with the airline, which can make changes and disruptions easier to manage.
  • Consider connecting through Asian hubs: Connecting through Southeast Asian hubs on low-cost carriers can sometimes cut long-haul fares compared with a single-airline ticket.
  • Avoid last-minute and peak-period bookings: Booking domestic flights a few weeks ahead and international flights a few months ahead is a sensible rule of thumb – fares vary by route and season.

Finding affordable airfares from Australia is a unique challenge. Long distances, fuel costs and limited domestic competition all push ticket prices up. Whether you're planning a quick interstate trip or a multi-stop overseas holiday, knowing how to find cheap flights comes down to the right search tools, good timing and smart booking techniques – not luck.

Quick answers

  • Best tool for discovering cheap flights: Use Google Flights or Skyscanner to set price alerts and compare dates, then check the price on the airline's own website before you buy.
  • When to book domestic flights: A few weeks ahead is a good general target – very last-minute fares are often the most expensive, especially in busy periods.
  • How to lower international flight costs: Compare connecting itineraries through Southeast Asian hubs like Singapore and Kuala Lumpur, including low-cost options, against direct and full-service fares.

Why domestic airfares are high in Australia

Flying within or out of Australia has become more expensive for structural reasons. Australia is a long way from major global flight corridors, which means longer flights and higher operating costs for international airlines. On domestic routes, limited competition adds extra pricing pressure.

In its December 2025 domestic airline competition report, the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) said the Qantas Group and Virgin Australia together serve about 98% of the domestic airline market. The ACCC also noted that high barriers to entry have helped keep out new competitors, particularly since Rex withdrew from routes between major cities. (Budget newcomer Bonza also stopped flying in 2024.) With so little competition on the main capital-city routes, fares tend to stay high, especially in busy periods.

Demand matters too. The ACCC reported that in October 2025 – a very busy month for domestic travel – 84.4% of seats were filled, compared with a 12-month average of 81.6%, and average airfares were the highest since December 2022. Finding cheap flights from Australia means working around these capacity squeezes and using a structured approach to spot real value.

How airline pricing pushes fares up

Understanding why prices move is the first step to beating the system. Airlines use revenue management systems that adjust fares based on factors such as:

  1. How fast seats are selling: As cheaper seats on a flight sell, the remaining seats are generally offered at higher prices.
  2. How close you are to departure: Fares booked shortly before departure are often higher, as airlines target travellers who have to fly on specific dates.
  3. Seasonal demand: Flights around school holidays, Easter and Christmas are often close to full, so airlines have little reason to discount.
  4. Fuel and operating costs: Changes in jet fuel prices affect fares, especially on long-haul routes such as flights to Europe and North America.

Many travellers overpay simply because they lock in their dates before checking nearby days. Shifting your departure by even a day or two can sometimes make a real difference. To understand how fares change across the year, read our guide on when the cheapest time to fly from Australia is.

Search tools and tactics that cut airfare costs

Airline pricing is complex, but the right tools and tactics can help you find lower fares.

1. Make the most of flight search engines

Flight comparison sites scan many airlines and travel agents to give you a broad view of available fares.

  • Google Flights: Its date grid and price insights show whether a current fare looks low, typical or high for that route. Turn on price tracking to get email alerts when prices change.
  • Skyscanner: Handy if you're flexible on where you go. Use the "Everywhere" search to find the cheapest destinations for your dates.
  • Consider booking direct: Comparison sites are great for discovery, but booking directly with the airline can make it simpler to manage changes or cancellations, and helps make sure your frequent flyer points are recorded properly. If you do use a third-party agent, check its fees and change rules first.

2. Get to know airline sales cycles

Australian airlines regularly run sales to fill seats in advance. Jetstar, for example, runs its Friday Fare Frenzy with discounted domestic and short-haul international fares. Subscribing to airline newsletters and turning on app notifications helps you hear about sales before the cheapest seats sell out. For more, see our breakdown of Jetstar sale tips.

3. Route through Asian hubs

For long-haul trips, a single ticket on a full-service airline isn't always the cheapest option. Australia is relatively close to major Asian hubs such as Singapore and Kuala Lumpur, which opens up connecting options.

You can fly to a major Asian hub, then connect on a separate ticket with a regional low-cost airline (such as AirAsia, Scoot or VietJet) to your final destination. Be aware that with separate tickets, the second airline usually isn't responsible if you miss the connection, so allow a generous buffer. If you're heading to Asia, learn how to build cost-effective itineraries in our guide to finding cheap flights to Asia from Australia.

4. Use frequent flyer programs strategically

Qantas Frequent Flyer and Virgin Australia Velocity points can soften the blow of high fares, particularly in peak periods.

  • Reward seats: Using points for reward seats often gives better value during peak holiday periods when cash fares soar – but taxes and carrier charges still apply, and availability is limited.
  • Transfer bonuses: Credit card and bank reward programs sometimes run promotions that give bonus points when you transfer to an airline program, which can reduce the points you need. Check the terms of each offer.
