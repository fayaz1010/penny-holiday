To score cheap flights from Australia, start watching international fares a few months before you travel, use flexible-date search tools across several departure cities, and keep an eye on airline sales. Combining low-cost connections through South-East Asian hubs with price alerts helps you get around some of the extra cost of flying from the bottom of the world.

Flying out of Australia has its own challenges: long distances, long flights and a highly concentrated domestic airline market. But understanding how airlines manage capacity, price seats through the seasons and charge for extras gives you a real advantage. Scoring a great fare isn't about luck – it's about following a system. Here's your checklist for finding cheap flights from Australia.

Quick answers

  • When should I book? As a rule of thumb, book domestic flights a few weeks to a couple of months ahead and international flights a few months ahead. Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report found that booking economy flights four to six weeks before departure saved international travellers an average of $140 compared with booking six months out. Book earlier for school holidays and Christmas.
  • Which days are cheapest to fly? Expedia's 2026 report named Friday the cheapest day to fly, saving up to 12% compared with Sunday. Tuesday was the quietest day. Results vary by route, so always compare a few dates.
  • How do I avoid surprise fees? Check the total cost – including bags, seats and any payment fee – before you pay, especially on low-cost airlines.

1. Get your booking timing right

Timing your purchase well is one of the most effective ways to cut costs. There's no single perfect booking date, but very last-minute fares are often expensive, and booking too early can mean missing later sales. For more on seasonal patterns, see our guide on when the cheapest time to fly from Australia is.

  • [ ] Set price alerts several months out for international trips. Automated alerts on Google Flights or Skyscanner let you track fare changes without searching every day. Airlines change prices as seats sell, so fare drops can appear without any announcement.

  • [ ] Look at shoulder periods. Flights are fullest – and fares usually highest – around Christmas, January and school holidays. Travelling outside these peaks generally gives you a better chance of finding discounted seats.

  • [ ] Use whole-month calendar views before you lock in dates. Shifting your departure by a day or two can sometimes make a big difference on long-haul routes. Flexible date grids help you spot cheaper days.

  • [ ] Compare different days of the week. Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report found Friday was the cheapest day to fly, but the best day depends on your route and season, so compare a few options.

  • [ ] Avoid school holiday peaks where you can. Fares often rise around state school holidays. If you must travel near a holiday period, compare dates just before and after the holidays start – they can sometimes be cheaper than peak days.

2. Understand airline competition and routes

Australia's domestic airline market is highly concentrated. In its December 2025 report, the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) said the Qantas Group (Qantas and Jetstar) and Virgin Australia together serve about 98% of the domestic market. It noted that high barriers to entry have helped keep out new competitors, particularly since Rex withdrew from routes between major cities. That means domestic fares on busy routes such as Sydney–Melbourne and Brisbane–Sydney reward careful planning.

International competition is much stronger. According to BITRE, 62 international airlines operated scheduled services to and from Australia in July 2026 (including three dedicated freight airlines), giving travellers plenty of options to compare. For more strategies, see our guide on how to find cheap flights.

  • [ ] Compare full-service fares against low-cost fares plus extras. Low-cost airlines advertise eye-catching base fares, but checked bags, extra carry-on weight and seat selection can add up quickly. Always compare the final total against full-service airlines, which often include checked bags, meals and entertainment in their economy fares.

  • [ ] Consider alternative airports at your destination. Some cities have more than one airport, and fares can differ between them. Compare all the airports that work for your trip, and factor in the cost and time of getting into town.

  • [ ] Search one-way tickets on different airlines. Booking two separate one-way tickets on different airlines can sometimes beat a standard return fare. You might fly Jetstar to South-East Asia and return on Scoot or Singapore Airlines, depending on sales. Just remember that each ticket is separate – if one flight changes, the other airline isn't obliged to help.

  • [ ] Watch domestic routes during sales. Even with limited competition, airline sales still pop up on busy domestic routes between Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and Perth. Watching for flash sales can help you snap up cheap domestic flights, including flights to a bigger international gateway.

  • [ ] Consider a positioning flight for long-haul trips. If international flights from your home airport are expensive, check whether a cheap domestic flight to another gateway, such as Sydney or Melbourne, brings your total cost down. Allow plenty of time between flights.

3. Use budget airline sales and keep extras under control

Low-cost carriers are a big part of Australia's international market. According to BITRE, low-cost carriers – AirAsia Berhad, AirAsia X, Cebu Pacific Air, Indonesia AirAsia, Jetstar, Scoot, T'Way Air and VietJet Air – carried 20.9% of all international passengers to and from Australia in July 2026. Knowing how to buy during their sales is key to getting the best value.

For specific tactics, see our guide to Jetstar sale tips.

  • [ ] Sign up for airline sale alerts. Airlines often announce sales to subscribers and members first – Club Jetstar members, for example, get early access to selected sales. Joining mailing lists for airlines like Jetstar, Scoot, AirAsia and Virgin Australia helps you get in before the cheapest seats go.

  • [ ] Check carry-on limits before you get to the airport. Budget airlines strictly enforce carry-on limits (often around 7kg for basic fares). Paying for extra bags or weight at the airport is usually far more expensive than adding them online when you book.

  • [ ] Skip paid seat selection unless you need it. Unless you're travelling with young kids or need a particular seat, you can usually skip paid seat selection – the airline will assign a seat, generally at check-in. Families should check the airline's policy on seating children with adults.

  • [ ] Bring your own snacks, an empty water bottle and downloaded entertainment. Budget airlines charge for food, drinks and Wi-Fi on board. Bringing your own snacks and filling a water bottle after security can save a surprising amount on medium-haul flights.

  • [ ] Compare booking currencies on overseas airline websites. When booking with an overseas airline such as VietJet or AirAsia, it can be worth comparing the price in AUD and in the airline's local currency. If you pay in a foreign currency, use a card with no foreign transaction fees.

4. Use Asian hubs and regional low-cost airlines

South-East Asia is a natural springboard for Australians heading to Europe, North America or North Asia. Hubs such as Singapore (SIN), Kuala Lumpur (KUL) and Ho Chi Minh City (SGN) offer wide onward networks. Splitting your trip into two legs – Australia to Asia, then Asia to your final destination – can sometimes cost much less than a single long-haul ticket.

For a full breakdown of routes and airlines, read our guide to finding cheap flights to Asia from Australia.

  • [ ] Compare self-transfer itineraries through Asian hubs. Instead of a single through-ticket from Sydney or Melbourne to Europe, compare a low-cost flight to Singapore or Kuala Lumpur followed by a separate long-haul flight.

  • [ ] Leave a long buffer for self-transfers. On separate tickets you may need to clear immigration, collect your bags and check in again. Build in a generous buffer – several hours, or even an overnight stop – because airlines aren't obliged to rebook you if you miss a connection on a separate ticket. Check whether you need a visa to enter the transit country.

  • [ ] Explore regional low-cost carriers within Asia. Airlines like VietJet, Scoot, AirAsia and Cebu Pacific fly extensive networks across Asia. Pairing them with flights from Australia opens up flexible multi-city trips.

  • [ ] Turn a long layover into a bonus stopover. Some airlines allow stopovers at their hubs, and choosing a longer connection can let you explore an extra city. Check the fare rules and any visa requirements.

  • [ ] Consider the weather. Wet seasons and quieter periods at Asian destinations can bring lower airfares and accommodation prices – just weigh up the weather risks.

5. Understand taxes, charges and payment fees

Knowing what's built into your fare helps you spot genuine deals and avoid unnecessary costs. Every international passenger leaving Australia pays government charges, the best known being the Passenger Movement Charge (PMC).

According to the Australian Border Force, the PMC is currently $70 per person departing Australia for another country. It's normally collected when your ticket is sold and included in the price. The Government has proposed raising it to $80 from 1 January 2027 through the Passenger Movement Charge Amendment Bill 2026, but as of late October 2026 that bill had not passed Parliament – so the increase isn't law yet.

  • [ ] Check for payment fees at checkout. Some airlines charge a fee for certain payment methods, such as card payments or buy-now-pay-later. Check the final price for each payment option before you pay.

  • [ ] Make sure taxes and charges are included in a "too good to be true" fare. If you spot an unusually cheap international fare, check that all taxes and charges, including the PMC, are in the total before you confirm. In Australia, businesses must generally show the total price when advertising.

  • [ ] Watch for foreign transaction fees on overseas booking sites. If you book with an overseas airline or travel agent and pay in a foreign currency, your card may charge a foreign transaction fee (often around 3% on standard cards). A card with no foreign transaction fees avoids this.

  • [ ] Check third-party booking site fees. Comparison sites like Skyscanner and Kayak are great for finding fares, but some lesser-known online travel agents charge extra fees for changes or customer service. Where possible, find the fare on a comparison site and then check the price on the airline's own website.

  • [ ] Buy travel insurance as soon as you book non-refundable flights. Once you've snagged a bargain, consider buying travel insurance straight away. Depending on your policy, it may protect your non-refundable fare if illness or other covered events force you to cancel.
